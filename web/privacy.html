Privacy policy

Effective October 31, 2026

NoteSpark is an offline notes app. It has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Everything you write stays on your phone. This policy explains what the app stores and where.

What NoteSpark stores

  • • Your notes: the title, text, format, whether they are pinned, and the dates they were created and edited, and your scratchpad. They are kept in a database on your device, one for each profile.
  • • A search index: for search by meaning, each note is turned into a list of numbers by a small model that is part of the app and runs on your device. The index is kept in the same database as your notes.
  • • Images you add from your phone are copied into NoteSpark's own storage on your device, so they keep showing if the original is moved or your phone clears its cache. They aren't uploaded. A copy is deleted once no note uses it.
  • • Your profile names, which profile is open, your theme choice, whether you've finished the welcome screens, and a list of your last 20 imports and exports. These are also kept on your device.
  • • Profile PINs: only a scrambled form of each PIN (a salted hash) is kept, in Android's secure storage. The PIN itself isn't stored, so it can't be recovered if you forget it. A profile with a PIN also has its notes encrypted on your phone, with a key kept in Android's secure storage.
  • • A copy of each PIN's hash, and of the profile's key wrapped with that PIN, is also kept in the app's ordinary storage so a restored phone can unlock the profile. Android's backup can include it, so someone who can read a backup could try every 4-digit PIN.
  • • The computer you paired for sync: its name, its public key and its addresses, plus this phone's own sync key, kept in Android's secure storage. No note text is stored for this.
  • • Documents you attach to a note (up to 32 MB each) are copied into NoteSpark's own storage and stored encrypted. They are not uploaded. A copy is deleted once no note uses it.
  • • A deleted profile stays on your device for 30 days so you can restore it, then it and its notes are erased.

None of this is sent to the developer or anyone else.

Nothing leaves your device

NoteSpark stays offline by design. It makes no requests to the internet of its own: no analytics, no crash reporting and no update checks. App updates come only through the store you installed it from. Sync is off until you turn it on, and then nothing leaves your own network (see Sync between your devices).

  • • Web images are off by default. If you turn on Show web images in Settings, a picture that a note links from a web address is requested from that website the first time you view the note, so that website sees your connection. It is then kept on your phone so it shows offline, even if the website removes it. Turn it off and no website is contacted. A saved copy is deleted once no note uses it and it hasn't been viewed for 30 days.
  • • When you export or share notes, the file goes only where you choose in the share sheet.
  • • Automatic backups, if you turn them on, are saved only to the folder you pick. Profiles with a PIN are left out unless you include them; backup files can be read without the PIN.
  • • Android's own backup: if backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may copy NoteSpark's data, including your notes, to your Google account, so it can be restored on a new phone. This is a feature of Android that you control in your phone's settings; NoteSpark doesn't send anything itself.

Sync between your devices

Sync is off for every profile until you turn it on in Settings under Sync and pair this phone with your computer.

  • • When it is on, this phone talks only to the computer you paired, at its address on your own network: your home or office Wi-Fi, a hotspot, USB tethering or a VPN. Everything is encrypted so nobody on the network can read it, and NoteSpark never connects to a public internet address. Nothing leaves your own network.
  • • There is no account and no server of ours. This phone's sync key stays in Android's secure storage and is left out of Android backups, so a restored phone has to pair again.
  • • A computer you did not pair gets no answer, and this phone ignores anything that is not from the computer it paired with.
  • • A sync file, if you export one, is saved or shared only where you choose and is locked with a passphrase that NoteSpark never stores. NoteSpark sends it nowhere; you move it yourself. Without the passphrase the notes in it cannot be read.
  • • A PIN is never synced. It protects only the device where you set it: the notes of a protected profile reach the other device as ordinary notes, and whether the profile there has a PIN is up to you.
  • • Turn sync off for a profile, or forget the computer, at any time. Notes already on this phone stay on it.

Permissions

  • • Notifications: to tell you things like an export finishing, and to remind you of tasks on their due date. These notifications are created and scheduled on your device; NoteSpark doesn't use push notifications. Reminders for profiles with a PIN don't show the task text.
  • • Photos: only the images you pick when adding an image to a note.
  • • Files: only the backup or note files you pick when importing notes, and the folder you pick for automatic backups.
  • • Fingerprint: only if you turn on fingerprint unlock for a profile. Android checks your fingerprint; NoteSpark never sees or stores it.
  • • Network access: to show images from web addresses that you add to notes (only if you turn on Show web images), and, if you turn on sync, to talk to your own computer on your own network. NoteSpark also allows plain http web addresses for those images, so use https addresses when you can.
  • • Camera: only while you scan a pairing code on the Sync screen. NoteSpark asks when you tap Scan, records and saves nothing, and you can paste the code instead.
  • • Running in the background: only while sync is on, so your devices stay up to date when NoteSpark is closed. Android shows a notification while it runs. If you say no, sync works while NoteSpark is open.

Your control over your data

  • • Move a note to the bin from its menu, or every note from Settings. Notes in the bin are erased for good 30 days later, or straight away if you delete them forever or empty the bin in Settings, under Bin.
  • • Export a copy of your notes at any time in Settings, under Import and export.
  • • Uninstalling NoteSpark deletes all of its data from your device. The developer has no copy, so deleted notes can't be recovered unless you exported them or Android's backup restores them.

Children

NoteSpark doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.
